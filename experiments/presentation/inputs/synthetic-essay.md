# The Lamp-Maker's Ledger

The lamp-maker kept two ledgers -- one for oil, one for promises -- and she never let the second outrun the first.[^1] Her apprentice called it "the arithmetic of patience"; she called it 'bookkeeping'.

## What the ledger held

Each page had three columns:

1. the date, written as 1873-04-02 rather than "April 2nd";
2. the quantity, in gills... or, when the cask ran low, in guesses;
3. a remark, often only an em dash --- meaning *nothing worth saying*.

> A lamp that burns too bright tonight is dark by Thursday.
> --- a saying the apprentice attributed to her, though she denied it

She kept the books in plain ink and wrote `TOTAL` in capitals at the foot of every page.[^2]

## Why it mattered

The town's [lending library](https://example.org/library) kept a copy of the last ledger. Nobody could say whether it was the oil or the promises they wanted to read.

[^1]: The apprentice's later account says "three ledgers", but no third one survives.
[^2]: Capitals were her only flourish -- the rest was ruled lines and 'ditto' marks.
